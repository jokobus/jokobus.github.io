What is OpenPGP?

Unfortunately, our emails are currently not encrypted by default. It is therefore very likely that government agencies (NSA and co.) and hackers can read our emails. This is not a desirable state of affairs. Encrypting email content with OpenPGP (PGP stands for ‘Pretty Good Privacy’) provides a remedy. A public key, with which the message is encrypted, and a private (secret) key, with which the message can be decrypted, are generated. As the name suggests, the public key can be made accessible to everyone, because how else can others write to you in encrypted form? The private key, on the other hand, must be kept safe so that it does not fall into the wrong hands.

You can find more information on the OpenPGP website or read their wikipedia article.

How to use OpenPGP

Personally, I use Thunderbird to encrypt emails, but there are a whole range of options. It’s not too difficult to set up.

Some of the most popular solutions are mentioned below, but there are many more.

Windows

Outlook

Outlook does not support OpenPGP natively. But you can install the (open-source) plug-in Gpg4win.

Thunderbird

In Thunderbird, simply go to Settings>Account Settings>YOUR EMAIL ACCOUNT>End-To-End-Encryption. There you can import your private key or generate a new one.

Linux

The easiest way is to use Thunderbird.

Ubuntu:

sudo apt install thunderbird

Arch:

sudo pacman -S thunderbird

Mobile devices

All the common mobile apps such as Outlook, Apple’s mail client, Gmail, Blue Mail etc. do not support OpenPGP. But no worries, there are excellent alternatives.

Android

I am personally a big fan of K-9 Mail. To use OpenPGP you need to install the free open-source apps K-9 Mail and OpenKeychain. First, you import your private key into OpenKeychain, then you go to the Account Settings of K-9 Mail. There you choose your Email-Account, click on “Cryptography” and select OpenKeychain as your OpenPGP Provider. That’s it.

iOS

Propably the free and open-source app PGPro is the best choice. However, I never tested this app, so I can’t give any recommendation.

Authentification

You can also let the governikus GmbH (owned by BSI Bundesamt für Sicherheit in der Informationstechnik, the german ministry for safety in information technology) authenticate your public keys. Follow the steps on their website.
