Disclaimer
These are practical personal notes and reference guidelines on the European Union / German Federal Aviation Office (LBA) drone regulations for operating Unmanned Aircraft Systems (UAS). Always cross-reference current national and EASA legislation before flying. Official training material: lba-openuav.de.

Quick Launch Checklist

Before operating any unmanned aircraft in European airspace, verify that you have completed all four prerequisites:

  1. Remote Pilot Competency (Drone License): Proof of competence (A1/A3) or remote pilot certificate (A2).
  2. UAS Operator Registration (e-ID): Register as an operator with your national aviation authority (e.g. LBA in Germany) and affix your Operator ID (e-ID) to the aircraft.
  3. Third-Party Liability Insurance: Mandatory aviation liability insurance that explicitly covers drone operations and potential damage.
  4. Category & Class Verification: Ensure your flight envelope (weight, location, distance to uninvolved people) matches your drone class and certified category.
💡
Fast-Track for Beginners
For standard camera drones and hobby operations, visit lba-openuav.de, complete the interactive online training, pass the free multiple-choice test for the A1/A3 certificate, and register as an operator in one seamless session.

Core Regulatory Terms & Flight Rules

  • Visual Line of Sight (VLOS): The remote pilot must maintain continuous, unaided visual contact with the unmanned aircraft (eyeglasses and contact lenses permitted, but no binoculars or FPV goggles alone).
  • FPV Flights (First Person View): Permitted only when accompanied by a designated competent spotter standing directly next to the pilot, maintaining continuous direct visual sight and verbally alerting the pilot to approaching aircraft or obstacles.
  • Maximum Take-Off Mass (MTOM): The certified total weight of the aircraft at launch, including batteries, cameras, and any additional payload.
  • Payload: Any components or gear not strictly necessary for flight mechanics (such as gimbals, external sensors, landing gear) are considered payload.
  • Maximum Flight Altitude: Standard flights in the Open Category are capped at 120 m AGL (Above Ground Level).

The Open Category: Subcategories A1, A2, and A3

1. Subcategory A1 (Fly over people, not crowds)

  • Permitted Aircraft: Certified C0 (< 250 g) and C1 (< 900 g), or legacy drones < 250 g.
  • Operational Limits: Overflight of uninvolved single individuals should be minimized; overflight of assemblies/crowds is strictly prohibited.
  • Licensing: C0 / < 250 g requires no exam (familiarity with manual); C1 requires A1/A3 Proof of Competence.

2. Subcategory A2 (Fly close to people)

  • Permitted Aircraft: Certified C2 (< 4 kg) or legacy transition models.
  • Operational Limits: Maintain a safe horizontal distance of at least 30 m from uninvolved persons (or 5 m in active low-speed mode $< 3\text{ m/s}$).
  • Licensing: Requires the A2 Remote Pilot Certificate (“Großer Drohnenführerschein”), practical self-training, and a proctored theoretical examination.

3. Subcategory A3 (Fly far from people)

  • Permitted Aircraft: Certified C3, C4, all aircraft up to 25 kg, and all scratch-built / DIY drones.
  • Operational Limits: Operations strictly restricted to open rural areas at least 150 m away from residential, commercial, industrial, or recreational areas. No uninvolved persons may be endangered within the buffer zone.

Rules for Scratch-Built & DIY Drones

▸ Operational Rules for Custom-Built FPV & DIY Multirotors

Custom, self-built drones without CE class identification labels fall under specific provisions depending on your pilot credentials:

  1. Without Pilot License (Only Drones < 250 g MTOM):

    • Pre-built commercial drones under 250 g without camera can be flown without registration.
    • If equipped with a sensor capable of recording personal data (such as a camera), operator registration is mandatory regardless of weight.
    • Any scratch-built drone exceeding 250 g strictly requires an aviation license.

  2. With Small License (A1/A3 Proof of Competence):

    • DIY drones with an MTOM between 250 g and 25 kg must be operated exclusively under Subcategory A3.
    • Flights permitted only in open, unpopulated fields.
    • Maintain a minimum distance of 150 m from residential, commercial, or public recreational zones.
    • Maintain a 1:1 height-to-distance buffer rule relative to nearby infrastructure.

  3. With Remote Pilot Certificate (A2):

    • Certain legacy and custom configurations up to 4 kg may be operated with reduced buffer distances under strict safety assessments.

Summary Checklist for Flight Day

  • Drone battery charged and airframe structurally verified
  • Proof of competence and Operator ID (e-ID) accessible on mobile / printed
  • Airspace zones verified via the German Digital Air Platform (DIPUL / DFS App)
  • Weather, wind speeds, and Kp index (solar geomagnetic activity) checked
  • Designated takeoff/landing zone secured without spectator obstruction
