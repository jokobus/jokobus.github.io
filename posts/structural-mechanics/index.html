This compendium synthesizes two core pillars: Part I: Continuum Mechanics and Three-Dimensional Linear Elasticity, and Part II: Structural Elements (Beams, Plates, Shells, Torsion) and Variational Principles.

Part I: Continuum Mechanics & Linear Elasticity

1. Tensor Calculus Foundations

Scalars, Vectors, and Tensors

In continuum mechanics, physical quantities must remain invariant under coordinate rotations [timoshenko1970] :

  • Scalars (0th-order): Magnitude only (e.g., mass density $\rho$, temperature $\theta$).
  • Vectors (1st-order): Magnitude and spatial orientation. Standard decomposition in Cartesian basis $\{\bm{e}_1, \bm{e}_2, \bm{e}_3\}$: $$\bm{v} = v_i \bm{e}_i = v_1 \bm{e}_1 + v_2 \bm{e}_2 + v_3 \bm{e}_3$$
  • Second-Order Tensors: Linear operators mapping vectors to vectors: $$\bm{T}(\alpha \bm{u} + \beta \bm{v}) = \alpha \bm{T}\bm{u} + \beta \bm{T}\bm{v}$$
  • Action on Basis Vectors: $\bm{T}\bm{e}_i = T_{ji} \bm{e}_j$: $$\begin{aligned} \bm{T}\bm{e}_1 &= T_{11} \bm{e}_1 + T_{21} \bm{e}_2 + T_{31} \bm{e}_3 \\ \bm{T}\bm{e}_2 &= T_{12} \bm{e}_1 + T_{22} \bm{e}_2 + T_{32} \bm{e}_3 \\ \bm{T}\bm{e}_3 &= T_{13} \bm{e}_1 + T_{23} \bm{e}_2 + T_{33} \bm{e}_3 \end{aligned}$$
  • Component Matrix & Projection: $$[\bm{T}] = \begin{bmatrix} T_{11} & T_{12} & T_{13} \\ T_{21} & T_{22} & T_{23} \\ T_{31} & T_{32} & T_{33} \end{bmatrix} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad T_{ij} = \bm{e}_i \cdot \bm{T} \bm{e}_j, \quad \bm{T} = T_{ij} (\bm{e}_i \otimes \bm{e}_j)$$

Core Tensor Operations

  • Scalar Product (Vectors): $\bm{u} \cdot \bm{v} = u_i v_i = u_1 v_1 + u_2 v_2 + u_3 v_3$.
  • Vector / Cross Product: $\bm{u} \times \bm{v} = \epsilon_{ijk} u_i v_j \bm{e}_k$, where $\epsilon_{ijk}$ is the Levi-Civita permutation symbol.
  • Dyadic Product (Tensor Product): $(\bm{a} \otimes \bm{b})\bm{v} = (\bm{b} \cdot \bm{v})\bm{a}$, with components $[\bm{a} \otimes \bm{b}]_{ij} = a_i b_j$.
  • Tensor Inner Product / Double Contraction: $$\bm{S} : \bm{D} = \text{tr}(\bm{S}^T \bm{D}) = S_{ij} D_{ij}$$
  • Trace: $\text{tr}(\bm{T}) = T_{ii} = T_{11} + T_{22} + T_{33}$ (invariant under coordinate rotations).
  • Determinant: Frame-invariant scalar volume scaling factor: $$\det \bm{T} = \epsilon_{ijk} T_{1i} T_{2j} T_{3k} = \frac{1}{6} \epsilon_{ijk} \epsilon_{pqr} T_{ip} T_{jq} T_{kr}$$
  • Transpose: $\bm{u} \cdot \bm{T}^T \bm{v} = \bm{v} \cdot \bm{T} \bm{u}$, with components $[\bm{T}^T]_{ij} = T_{ji}$.
  • Symmetric and Skew-Symmetric Decomposition: $$\bm{T} = \bm{E} + \bm{W}, \quad \bm{E} = \frac{1}{2}(\bm{T} + \bm{T}^T), \quad \bm{W} = \frac{1}{2}(\bm{T} - \bm{T}^T)$$ Here $\bm{E}^T = \bm{E}$ is symmetric, and $\bm{W}^T = -\bm{W}$ is skew-symmetric. Any skew-symmetric tensor possesses an axial vector $\bm{w} = -\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk} W_{jk} \bm{e}_i$ such that $\bm{W}\bm{v} = \bm{w} \times \bm{v}$.
  • Positive Definiteness: A symmetric tensor $\bm{S}$ is positive definite if: $$\bm{v} \cdot \bm{S}\bm{v} > 0 \quad \forall \bm{v} \neq \bm{0}$$
  • Invertibility & Transpose Commutation: An invertible tensor satisfies $\bm{S}\bm{S}^{-1} = \bm{S}^{-1}\bm{S} = \bm{I}$, with: $$(\bm{S}^{-1})^T = (\bm{S}^T)^{-1} = \bm{S}^{-T}$$
  • Orthogonal & Rotation Tensors: $\bm{Q}^T = \bm{Q}^{-1}$, meaning $\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^T = \bm{I}$. Hence $\det(\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^T) = (\det\bm{Q})^2 = 1 \implies \det\bm{Q} = \pm 1$. A proper rotation tensor $\bm{R}$ preserves coordinate handedness: $\det\bm{R} = +1$.

Coordinate Transformations (Change of Basis)

When transforming from reference basis $\bm{e}_i$ to rotated basis $\bm{e}'_i = Q_{mi} \bm{e}_m$:

  • Transformation Matrix (Direction Cosines): $Q_{ij} = \bm{e}_i \cdot \bm{e}'_j$.
  • Basis Vectors: $\bm{e}'_i = \bm{Q} \bm{e}_i$ (if $\bm{Q}$ rotates the basis).
  • Vector Components: $[\bm{v}]_{\bm{e}'} = \bm{Q}^T [\bm{v}]_{\bm{e}}$, or in index notation: $v'_i = Q_{ki} v_k$.
  • Tensor Components: $[\bm{T}]_{\bm{e}'} = \bm{Q}^T [\bm{T}]_{\bm{e}} \bm{Q}$, or in index notation: $T'_{ij} = Q_{ki} T_{kl} Q_{lj}$.
  • Invariance: Invariants such as trace $I_1 = \text{tr}(\bm{T})$ and determinant $I_3 = \det(\bm{T})$ evaluate to identical numerical values in any rotated basis.

Eigenvalues and Principal Invariants

For any symmetric second-order tensor $\bm{S}$, the eigenvalue problem $\bm{S}\bm{n} = \lambda \bm{n}$ yields the characteristic polynomial:

$$\det(\bm{S} - \lambda \bm{I}) = 0 \quad \implies \quad \lambda^3 - I_1 \lambda^2 + I_2 \lambda - I_3 = 0$$

The three principal invariants are:

$$\begin{aligned} I_1(\bm{S}) &= \text{tr}(\bm{S}) = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 \\ I_2(\bm{S}) &= \frac{1}{2}\left[(\text{tr}\bm{S})^2 - \text{tr}(\bm{S}^2)\right] = \lambda_1\lambda_2 + \lambda_2\lambda_3 + \lambda_3\lambda_1 \\ I_3(\bm{S}) &= \det \bm{S} = \lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3 \end{aligned}$$
📝
Note
Extremal Normal Components: The largest and smallest principal values ($\lambda_{\max}, \lambda_{\min}$) correspond to the absolute maximum and minimum normal components that the tensor can exhibit along any coordinate direction in three-dimensional space.

Spectral decomposition allows any symmetric tensor to be represented along its orthogonal principal axes:

$$\bm{S} = \sum_{i=1}^3 \lambda_i (\bm{n}_i \otimes \bm{n}_i) = \bm{Q} \bm{\Lambda} \bm{Q}^T$$

where $\bm{\Lambda} = \text{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3)$ and $\bm{Q} = [\bm{n}_1, \bm{n}_2, \bm{n}_3]$ is the orthogonal modal matrix.

Scalar, Vector, and Tensor Fields

  • Differential of a Field: $d\phi = \phi(\bm{x} + d\bm{x}) - \phi(\bm{x}) \approx \nabla \phi \cdot d\bm{x}$.
  • Gradient of a Scalar: $\nabla \phi = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x_i} \bm{e}_i = \phi_{,i}$.
  • Gradient of a Vector: $\nabla \bm{v} = \frac{\partial v_i}{\partial x_j} \bm{e}_i \otimes \bm{e}_j = v_{i,j}$.
  • Divergence of a Vector: $\nabla \cdot \bm{v} = \text{tr}(\nabla \bm{v}) = v_{i,i} = \frac{\partial v_1}{\partial x_1} + \frac{\partial v_2}{\partial x_2} + \frac{\partial v_3}{\partial x_3}$.
  • Divergence of a Tensor: Defined through $(\nabla \cdot \bm{S}) \cdot \bm{a} = \nabla \cdot (\bm{S}^T \bm{a})$, yielding component form: $$(\nabla \cdot \bm{S})_i = S_{ij,j} = \frac{\partial S_{ij}}{\partial x_j}$$
  • Curl of a Vector: $\nabla \times \bm{v} = \epsilon_{ijk} v_{k,j} \bm{e}_i$.
  • Laplacian: $\Delta \phi = \nabla^2 \phi = \nabla \cdot (\nabla \phi) = \phi_{,ii} = \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x_1^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x_2^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x_3^2}$.

Integral Theorems

  • Divergence Theorem of Gauss: Relates a volume integral over domain $\mathcal{B}$ to a flux integral over boundary surface $\partial\mathcal{B}$: $$\int_{\partial \mathcal{B}} \bm{v} \cdot \bm{n} \, dA = \int_{\mathcal{B}} \nabla \cdot \bm{v} \, dV \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \int_{\partial \mathcal{B}} v_i \hat{n}_i \, dA = \int_{\mathcal{B}} v_{i,i} \, dV$$
  • Stokes’ Theorem: Relates a surface integral over surface $\Gamma$ to a contour line integral along bounding curve $C = \partial\Gamma$: $$\int_{\Gamma} (\nabla \times \bm{v}) \cdot \bm{n} \, dA = \oint_{C} \bm{v} \cdot \bm{t} \, ds \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \int_{\Gamma} \epsilon_{ijk} v_{k,j} \hat{n}_i \, dA = \oint_{C} v_i \hat{t}_i \, ds$$

2. Fundamentals of Linear Elasticity: Kinematics & Strain Measures

Kinematics: Material vs. Spatial Descriptions

Let $\bm{X}$ denote the reference (material) configuration and $\bm{x} = \bm{\phi}(\bm{X}, t)$ the deformed (spatial) configuration:

  • Invertible Motion: $\bm{X} = \bm{\phi}^{-1}(\bm{x}, t)$, valid if the Jacobian determinant satisfies: $$\det(\nabla_{\bm{X}} \bm{\phi}) = \det\left[\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial X_J}\right] > 0$$
  • Displacement Field:
    • Material: $\bm{U}(\bm{X}, t) = \bm{x} - \bm{X} = \bm{\phi}(\bm{X}, t) - \bm{X}$.
    • Spatial: $\bm{u}(\bm{x}, t) = \bm{x} - \bm{\phi}^{-1}(\bm{x}, t) = \bm{x} - \bm{X}$.
  • Velocity Field: $\bm{V}(\bm{X}, t) = \left.\frac{\partial \bm{\phi}}{\partial t}\right|_{\bm{X}} = \left.\frac{\partial \bm{U}}{\partial t}\right|_{\bm{X}}$.
  • Acceleration Field: $\bm{A}(\bm{X}, t) = \left.\frac{\partial^2 \bm{\phi}}{\partial t^2}\right|_{\bm{X}}$. In spatial coordinates: $$\bm{a}(\bm{x}, t) = \frac{\partial \bm{v}}{\partial t} + (\nabla \bm{v})\bm{v}$$
  • Material Time Derivative: $$\frac{D(\cdot)}{Dt} = \frac{\partial(\cdot)}{\partial t} + \bm{v} \cdot \nabla(\cdot) = \frac{\partial(\cdot)}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\cdot)}{\partial x_i} v_i$$

Deformation Gradient & Finite Strain Measures

  • Deformation Gradient Tensor: Maps infinitesimal material line segments $d\bm{X}$ to deformed spatial segments $d\bm{x} = \bm{F} d\bm{X}$: $$\bm{F} = \frac{\partial \bm{x}}{\partial \bm{X}} = \nabla_{\bm{X}} \bm{\phi}, \quad F_{ij} = \frac{\partial x_i}{\partial X_j} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial x_1}{\partial X_1} & \frac{\partial x_1}{\partial X_2} & \frac{\partial x_1}{\partial X_3} \\ \frac{\partial x_2}{\partial X_1} & \frac{\partial x_2}{\partial X_2} & \frac{\partial x_2}{\partial X_3} \\ \frac{\partial x_3}{\partial X_1} & \frac{\partial x_3}{\partial X_2} & \frac{\partial x_3}{\partial X_3} \end{bmatrix}$$
  • Jacobian Determinant (Volume Ratio): $J = \det \bm{F} > 0$. Deformed volume element: $dv = J \, dV = \det \bm{F} \, dV$. The motion is isochoric (incompressible) if and only if $J = 1$.
  • Square of Differential Length Change: $$(dx)^2 - (dX)^2 = 2\, d\bm{X} \cdot \bm{E} \, d\bm{X} = 2\, d\bm{x} \cdot \bm{e} \, d\bm{x}$$
  • Right Cauchy-Green Tensor: $\bm{C} = \bm{F}^T \bm{F}$ (components $C_{IJ} = F_{kI} F_{kJ}$).
  • Green-Lagrange Strain Tensor: $$\bm{E} = \frac{1}{2}(\bm{C} - \bm{I}) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{U} + \nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{U}^T + \nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{U}^T \nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{U}\right)$$
  • Left Cauchy-Green Tensor (Finger Tensor): $\bm{b} = \bm{F} \bm{F}^T$ (components $b_{ij} = F_{iK} F_{jK}$), with inverse $\bm{b}^{-1} = \bm{F}^{-T} \bm{F}^{-1}$.
  • Euler-Almansi Strain Tensor: $\bm{e} = \frac{1}{2}(\bm{I} - \bm{b}^{-1})$.
  • Finite Engineering Strain: $\bm{\varepsilon}_{\text{finite}} = \frac{1}{2}(\bm{F} + \bm{F}^T) - \bm{I}$.

Mapping and Fiber Deformation

  1. Stretch Ratio $\lambda$ in Material Direction $\bm{N}$ ($\|\bm{N}\|=1$):
    • Compute deformation gradient $\bm{F} = \frac{\partial x_i}{\partial X_J}$.
    • Calculate deformed line vector: $d\bm{x} = \bm{F} (dX \bm{N})$.
    • Compute stretch: $$\lambda = \frac{\|d\bm{x}\|}{\|d\bm{X}\|} = \sqrt{\bm{N} \cdot \bm{C} \bm{N}}$$
  2. Angle $\theta$ Between Deformed Fibers Initially Along $(\bm{N}_1, \bm{N}_2)$:
    • Deformed segment vectors: $d\bm{x}^{(1)} = \bm{F} (dX_1 \bm{N}_1)$, $d\bm{x}^{(2)} = \bm{F} (dX_2 \bm{N}_2)$.
    • Deformed angle: $$\cos \theta = \frac{d\bm{x}^{(1)} \cdot d\bm{x}^{(2)}}{\|d\bm{x}^{(1)}\| \|d\bm{x}^{(2)}\|} = \frac{\bm{N}_1 \cdot \bm{C} \bm{N}_2}{\lambda_1 \lambda_2}$$

Infinitesimal Strain Tensor

Under the linearizing assumption of small displacement gradients ($\|\nabla \bm{u}\| \ll 1$), second-order displacement products vanish: $\bm{E} \approx \bm{e} \approx \bm{\varepsilon}$:

$$\boxed{\bm{\varepsilon} = \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\bm{u} + \nabla\bm{u}^T) \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \varepsilon_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}(u_{i,j} + u_{j,i})}$$
  • Displacement Field Types:
    • Infinitesimal Rigid Body Displacement: $\bm{u}(\bm{x}) = \bm{u}_0 + \bm{W}(\bm{x} - \bm{x}_0)$, where $\bm{W}$ is skew-symmetric ($\bm{\varepsilon} = \bm{0}$).
    • Homogeneous Displacement: $\bm{u}(\bm{x}) = \bm{u}_0 + \bm{A}(\bm{x} - \bm{x}_0)$, where $\bm{A}$ is a constant second-order tensor (yielding constant strain $\bm{\varepsilon}$).
  • Normal Strains (Geometric Meaning): For an element $d\bm{X} = dS \bm{n}$, deformed length $ds$: $$\frac{ds - dS}{dS} = \bm{n} \cdot \bm{\varepsilon} \bm{n} = \varepsilon_{nn}$$ Hence, $\varepsilon_{11}, \varepsilon_{22}, \varepsilon_{33}$ represent unit fractional elongations along axes $x_1, x_2, x_3$.
  • Shear Strains (Geometric Meaning): For orthogonal elements $d\bm{X}^{(1)} = dS_1 \bm{m}$ and $d\bm{X}^{(2)} = dS_2 \bm{n}$ ($\bm{m} \cdot \bm{n} = 0$): $$d\bm{x}^{(1)} \cdot d\bm{x}^{(2)} = ds_1 ds_2 \cos \phi \approx 2\, dS_1 dS_2 \varepsilon_{mn}$$ The decrease in the right angle between them defines the engineering shear strain $\gamma$: $$\gamma = \frac{\pi}{2} - \phi = 2\varepsilon_{mn} = 2(\bm{m} \cdot \bm{\varepsilon} \bm{n})$$ Therefore, $2\varepsilon_{ij} \; (i \neq j)$ is the decrease in the right angle between fibers initially aligned with axes $x_i$ and $x_j$.
  • Volumetric Strain: $e = \text{tr}(\bm{\varepsilon}) = \varepsilon_{kk} = \varepsilon_{11} + \varepsilon_{22} + \varepsilon_{33} \approx \frac{dv - dV}{dV} = \frac{\Delta V}{V_0}$.
  • Compression Criterion: A continuum volume experiences compressive contraction along some direction if and only if at least one eigenvalue of $\varepsilon_{ij}$ is negative.
  • Step-by-Step Deformation Procedure:
    1. Given motion $\bm{x}(\bm{X})$ and initial fibers $d\bm{X}^{(k)} = dS_k \bm{N}_k$.
    2. Compute deformation gradient: $F_{ij} = \frac{\partial x_i}{\partial X_j}$.
    3. Determine deformed fiber vectors: $d\bm{x}^{(k)} = \bm{F} d\bm{X}^{(k)}$.
    4. Evaluate stretches: $\lambda_k = \frac{\|d\bm{x}^{(k)}\|}{dS_k} = \sqrt{\bm{N}_k \cdot \bm{C} \bm{N}_k}$.
    5. Evaluate deformed angles: $\cos\phi = \frac{\bm{N}_1 \cdot \bm{C} \bm{N}_2}{\lambda_1 \lambda_2} \implies \text{angular change } \gamma = \frac{\pi}{2} - \phi$.

Saint-Venant Compatibility Conditions

To guarantee that a symmetric strain tensor field $\varepsilon_{ij}(\bm{x})$ can be integrated into a single-valued, continuous displacement field $\bm{u}(\bm{x})$ in a simply-connected domain, the Saint-Venant compatibility conditions must be satisfied:

$$\varepsilon_{ij,kl} + \varepsilon_{kl,ij} - \varepsilon_{ik,jl} - \varepsilon_{jl,ik} = 0$$

In 3D Cartesian coordinates, this expands to six independent equations:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{11}}{\partial x_2^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{22}}{\partial x_1^2} &= 2 \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{12}}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} \\ \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{22}}{\partial x_3^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{33}}{\partial x_2^2} &= 2 \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{23}}{\partial x_2 \partial x_3} \\ \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{33}}{\partial x_1^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{11}}{\partial x_3^2} &= 2 \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{13}}{\partial x_3 \partial x_1} \\ \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{11}}{\partial x_2 \partial x_3} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x_1} \left( -\frac{\partial \varepsilon_{23}}{\partial x_1} + \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{13}}{\partial x_2} + \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{12}}{\partial x_3} \right) \\ \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{22}}{\partial x_3 \partial x_1} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x_2} \left( \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{23}}{\partial x_1} - \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{13}}{\partial x_2} + \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{12}}{\partial x_3} \right) \\ \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{33}}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x_3} \left( \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{23}}{\partial x_1} + \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{13}}{\partial x_2} - \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{12}}{\partial x_3} \right) \end{aligned}$$
💡
Tip
Linear Strain Fields: If all components of $\varepsilon_{ij}$ are linear polynomials in coordinates $(x_1, x_2, x_3)$, all second spatial derivatives vanish identically, so compatibility is automatically satisfied.

3. Motion, Equilibrium & Conservation Laws

Cauchy Stress Principle & Traction

The continuum hypothesis assumes matter is continuously distributed, allowing internal force transmission across any arbitrary internal surface:

  • Forces in Continuum: Total forces consist of body forces (distributed throughout volume, e.g., gravity, inertia) and surface forces (tractions).
  • Traction Vector: Force per unit area acting on a surface cutting plane characterized by unit outward normal $\bm{n}$: $$\bm{t}_{\bm{n}} = \lim_{\Delta A \to 0} \frac{\Delta \bm{F}}{\Delta A} \quad \implies \quad \boxed{\bm{t}_{\bm{n}} = \bm{\sigma} \bm{n}}$$
  • Normal Traction Component: $$t_n = \bm{n} \cdot \bm{t}_{\bm{n}} = \bm{n} \cdot \bm{\sigma} \bm{n} \quad (t_n > 0 \text{ tensile}, \quad t_n < 0 \text{ compressive})$$
  • Shearing Traction Component: $$t_s = \sqrt{\|\bm{t}_{\bm{n}}\|^2 - t_n^2} = \sqrt{\|\bm{\sigma}\bm{n}\|^2 - (\bm{n} \cdot \bm{\sigma} \bm{n})^2}$$
  • Newton’s Third Law (Action-Reaction): $$\bm{t}_{-\bm{n}} = \lim_{\Delta A \to 0} \frac{-\Delta \bm{F}}{\Delta A} = -\bm{t}_{\bm{n}}$$
  • Maximum Shearing Stress: Determined from principal stresses $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \lambda_3$: $$\tau_{\max} = \frac{\lambda_{\max} - \lambda_{\min}}{2}$$ acting on cutting planes bisecting the principal stress axes $\bm{n}_{\lambda_{\max}}$ and $\bm{n}_{\lambda_{\min}}$.
  • Resultant Force and Moment on Surface Area $A$: $$\bm{F} = \int_A \bm{t}_{\bm{n}} \, dA, \quad \bm{M} = \int_A \bm{x} \times \bm{t}_{\bm{n}} \, dA$$

Cauchy Stress Tensor on a Differential Element

  • Stress Vectors on Coordinate Planes: $\bm{t}_{\bm{e}_j} = \bm{\sigma} \bm{e}_j$. For instance: $$\bm{t}_{\bm{e}_1} = \sigma_{11} \bm{e}_1 + \sigma_{21} \bm{e}_2 + \sigma_{31} \bm{e}_3$$
  • Index Convention: In $\sigma_{ij}$, index $j$ identifies the plane normal $\bm{e}_j$, while index $i$ indicates the direction of the stress vector component.
  • Normal Stress: $\sigma_{ii}$ (tensile when positive, compressive when negative).
  • Shear Stresses: $\sigma_{ij}$ with $i \neq j$. The resultant shear stress magnitude on coordinate face $\bm{e}_1$ is $|\tau_1| = \sqrt{\sigma_{21}^2 + \sigma_{31}^2}$.
  • Conservation of Angular Momentum: Enforcing zero net moment on the infinitesimal element yields the fundamental symmetry of the Cauchy stress tensor: $$\sigma_{ij} = \sigma_{ji} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \bm{\sigma} = \bm{\sigma}^T$$

Equations of Linear Momentum (Equilibrium)

Balancing forces on the differential volume yields the Navier-Cauchy equations of motion:

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \bm{\sigma} + \rho \bm{b} = \rho \ddot{\bm{u}} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \sigma_{ij,j} + \rho b_i = \rho \ddot{u}_i}$$

For static equilibrium:

$$\nabla \cdot \bm{\sigma} + \rho \bm{b} = \bm{0} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \sigma_{ij,j} + \rho b_i = 0$$

where $\bm{b}$ represents body forces per unit mass (gravity, magnetic fields). When body forces are negligible ($\bm{b} = \bm{0}$):

$$\sigma_{ij,j} = 0$$

4. Constitutive Laws & Linear Elastic Solids

Generalized Hooke’s Law & Symmetries

For linear elastic materials, the fourth-order elasticity tensor $\mathbb{C}$ relates stress to strain:

$$\bm{\sigma} = \mathbb{C} : \bm{\varepsilon} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \sigma_{ij} = C_{ijkl} \varepsilon_{kl}$$
  • Elastic Behavior Definition: (1) Stress $\bm{\sigma}$ is a unique one-to-one function of strain $\bm{\varepsilon}$; (2) Material returns entirely to its original configuration upon load removal.
  • Total Components: A general 4th-order tensor in 3D has $3^4 = 81$ components.
  • Minor Symmetries: Due to stress symmetry ($\sigma_{ij} = \sigma_{ji}$) and strain symmetry ($\varepsilon_{kl} = \varepsilon_{lk}$): $$C_{ijkl} = C_{jikl} = C_{ijlk} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{36 independent components}$$
  • Major Symmetry (Hyperelasticity): If a strain energy density potential $U(\bm{\varepsilon})$ exists such that $\sigma_{ij} = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \varepsilon_{ij}}$: $$C_{ijkl} = \frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial \varepsilon_{ij} \partial \varepsilon_{kl}} = C_{klij} \quad \implies \quad \textbf{21 independent components}$$

Internal Strain Energy Density $U$

Strain energy per unit volume $[J/\text{m}^3]$:

  • General Anisotropic Elastic Solid: $$U(\bm{\varepsilon}) = \frac{1}{2} C_{ijkl} \varepsilon_{ij} \varepsilon_{kl} = \frac{1}{2} \sigma_{ij} \varepsilon_{ij}$$
  • Isotropic Elastic Solid: $$\begin{aligned} U &= \frac{1}{2} \left(\lambda \varepsilon_{kk} \varepsilon_{ii} + 2\mu \varepsilon_{ij} \varepsilon_{ij}\right) \\ &= \frac{1}{2E} \left((1+\nu)\sigma_{ij}\sigma_{ij} - \nu \sigma_{kk}\sigma_{ii}\right) = \frac{\lambda - 2\mu}{2} (I_1^{(\bm{\varepsilon})})^2 + 2\mu I_2^{(\bm{\varepsilon})} \end{aligned}$$
  • Standard Loading Scenarios:
    • Uniaxial Tension: $U = \frac{\sigma_0^2}{2E}$
    • Pure Shear: $U = \frac{\tau_0^2}{2G}$
    • Hydrostatic Pressure: $U = \frac{p^2}{2K}$
  • Hyperelasticity: $\sigma_{ij} = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \varepsilon_{ij}}$ and $C_{ijkl} = \frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial \varepsilon_{ij} \partial \varepsilon_{kl}}$.
  • Material Stability: Thermodynamic stability requires $U(\bm{\varepsilon}) > 0$ for all $\bm{\varepsilon} \neq \bm{0} \implies \mathbb{C}$ is positive definite. This guarantees $E > 0$, $G > 0$, $K > 0$, and $-1 < \nu < 0.5$.
  • Homogeneity vs. Isotropy:
    • Homogeneous: Elastic moduli $C_{ijkl}$ are spatially uniform (independent of position $\bm{x}$).
    • Isotropic: Mechanical properties are direction-independent.

Isotropic Linear Elastic Solids

Isotropic materials require only two independent Lamé constants ($\lambda, \mu$):

$$\boxed{\sigma_{ij} = \lambda \delta_{ij} \varepsilon_{kk} + 2\mu \varepsilon_{ij}} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \bm{\sigma} = \lambda \text{tr}(\bm{\varepsilon})\bm{I} + 2\mu \bm{\varepsilon}$$

Alternative inverted forms:

$$\varepsilon_{ij} = \frac{1}{2\mu} \sigma_{ij} - \frac{\lambda}{2\mu} \varepsilon_{kk} \delta_{ij} = \frac{1}{2\mu} \sigma_{ij} - \frac{\lambda}{2\mu(3\lambda + 2\mu)} \sigma_{kk} \delta_{ij}$$

Inverted standard Hooke’s Law in terms of Young’s modulus $E$ and Poisson’s ratio $\nu$:

$$\boxed{\varepsilon_{ij} = \frac{1}{E}\left[(1+\nu)\sigma_{ij} - \nu \delta_{ij} \sigma_{kk}\right]} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \bm{\varepsilon} = \frac{1+\nu}{E}\bm{\sigma} - \frac{\nu}{E}\text{tr}(\bm{\sigma})\bm{I}$$

Hydrostatic & Deviatoric Decomposition

Any stress state can be decomposed into a pure volumetric (hydrostatic) and a shape-distorting (deviatoric) contribution:

$$\sigma_{ij} = H_{ij} + S_{ij}$$
  • Hydrostatic Stress (Volumetric): $$H_{ij} = \frac{1}{3} \sigma_{kk} \delta_{ij} = p_{\text{mean}} \delta_{ij}$$
  • Deviatoric Stress (Distortional): $$S_{ij} = \sigma_{ij} - H_{ij} = \sigma_{ij} - \frac{1}{3}\sigma_{kk}\delta_{ij}$$

Navier’s Displacement Equations

Substituting kinematic strain definitions and isotropic Hooke’s law directly into the momentum balance yields Navier’s equations:

$$\boxed{\mu u_{i,jj} + (\lambda + \mu) u_{j,ij} + \rho b_i = 0} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \mu \nabla^2 \bm{u} + (\lambda + \mu) \nabla(\nabla \cdot \bm{u}) + \rho \bm{b} = \bm{0}$$

Specific 3D Benchmark Formulations

  1. Uniaxial Extension of an Elastic Bar:
    • Stress state: $\sigma_{11} = \sigma_0$, all other $\sigma_{ij} = 0$.
    • Strains from Hooke’s Law: $\varepsilon_{11} = \frac{\sigma_0}{E}$, $\varepsilon_{22} = \varepsilon_{33} = -\nu \varepsilon_{11} = -\frac{\nu\sigma_0}{E}$.
    • Solution Verification Checklist:
      1. Equilibrium satisfied ($\sigma_{ij,j} + \rho b_i = 0$).
      2. Boundary conditions met on all surfaces ($\bm{t} = \bm{\sigma}\bm{n}$).
      3. Compatibility / integrability fulfilled ($\varepsilon_{ij}$ integrable to continuous displacement field $\bm{u}$).
  2. Pure Bending of a Prismatic Beam:
    • Moment couple $\bm{M}$ applied at ends; lateral surfaces traction-free; body forces $\rho \bm{b} = \bm{0}$.
    • Assumption: Only longitudinal stress develops $\implies \sigma_{11} \neq 0$, all other $\sigma_{ij} = 0$. Equilibrium requires $\frac{\partial\sigma_{11}}{\partial x_1} = 0$, hence $\sigma_{11} = \sigma_{11}(x_2, x_3)$.
    • Compatibility requires $\frac{\partial^2\sigma_{11}}{\partial x_k^2} = 0 \; (k=2,3) \implies \sigma_{11}$ must be a linear function: $$\sigma_{11} = \alpha + \beta x_2 + \gamma x_3$$
    • For centroidal axes: $\alpha = 0$. Along principal axes of inertia ($I_{23} = 0$): $$\sigma_{11} = -\frac{M_3}{I_{33}} x_2 + \frac{M_2}{I_{22}} x_3$$
    • Strain-Curvature Relation: $\varepsilon_{11} = -y \kappa = \frac{\sigma_{11}}{E}$, with transverse contraction $\varepsilon_{22} = \varepsilon_{33} = -\nu \varepsilon_{11}$.

5. Two-Dimensional Continua & 1D Trusses

Plane Strain Formulation

Applies to long cylindrical/prismatic bodies along $x_3$ with transverse loading (e.g., dams, tunnels, retaining walls):

  • Kinematic Hypotheses: $\varepsilon_{13} = \varepsilon_{23} = \varepsilon_{33} = 0$, $u_3 = 0$.
  • All quantities depend only on in-plane coordinates: $\bm{u}, \bm{\sigma}, \bm{\varepsilon} = f(x_1, x_2) \neq f(x_1, x_2, x_3)$.
  • Out-of-Plane Stress: Restraining out-of-plane expansion induces normal stress: $$\sigma_{33} = \nu(\sigma_{11} + \sigma_{22}) \neq 0$$

Plane Stress Formulation

Applies to thin plates and membranes (thickness $h$ along $x_3$) loaded exclusively in their plane $(x_1, x_2)$:

  • Stress Hypotheses: $\sigma_{33} = \sigma_{13} = \sigma_{23} = 0$.
  • In-Plane Strains: $$\varepsilon_{11} = \frac{1}{E}(\sigma_{11} - \nu \sigma_{22}), \quad \varepsilon_{22} = \frac{1}{E}(\sigma_{22} - \nu \sigma_{11}), \quad \varepsilon_{12} = \frac{1+\nu}{E}\sigma_{12}$$
  • Out-of-Plane Strain: Free lateral Poisson contraction/expansion occurs: $$\varepsilon_{33} = -\frac{\nu}{E}(\sigma_{11} + \sigma_{22}) \neq 0$$
  • Constitutive Inversion: $$\sigma_{ij} = \frac{E}{1-\nu^2}\left[(1-\nu)\varepsilon_{ij} + \nu \delta_{ij}\varepsilon_{kk}\right] \quad (i,j = 1,2)$$

Comparative Summary: Plane Strain vs. Plane Stress

$$\begin{array}{l|c|c} \textbf{Property} & \textbf{Plane Strain (Dams, Tunnels, Thick)} & \textbf{Plane Stress (Thin Plates, Membranes)} \\ \hline \textbf{Kinematic Constraint} & \varepsilon_{33} = \varepsilon_{13} = \varepsilon_{23} = 0 & \varepsilon_{13} = \varepsilon_{23} = 0, \; \varepsilon_{33} \neq 0 \\ \textbf{Stress Constraint} & \sigma_{13} = \sigma_{23} = 0, \; \sigma_{33} \neq 0 & \sigma_{33} = \sigma_{13} = \sigma_{23} = 0 \\ \textbf{Out-of-Plane Component} & \sigma_{33} = \nu(\sigma_{11} + \sigma_{22}) & \varepsilon_{33} = -\frac{\nu}{E}(\sigma_{11} + \sigma_{22}) \\ \textbf{Equivalent Moduli} & \bar{E} = \frac{E}{1-\nu^2}, \; \bar{\nu} = \frac{\nu}{1-\nu} & E, \; \nu \text{ (Standard Hooke's Law)} \end{array}$$

Airy Stress Functions

In 2D elastostatics with constant or zero body forces, linear momentum equilibrium:

$$\frac{\partial \sigma_{11}}{\partial x_1} + \frac{\partial \sigma_{12}}{\partial x_2} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial \sigma_{12}}{\partial x_1} + \frac{\partial \sigma_{22}}{\partial x_2} = 0$$

is identically satisfied by introducing a scalar Airy stress function $\Phi(x_1, x_2)$:

$$\boxed{\sigma_{11} = \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2}, \quad \sigma_{22} = \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2}, \quad \sigma_{12} = -\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2}}$$

Substituting these stress components into the Saint-Venant strain compatibility condition reveals that $\Phi$ must satisfy the Biharmonic Equation:

$$\boxed{\nabla^4 \Phi = \frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_1^4} + 2\frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2 \partial x_2^2} + \frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_2^4} = 0}$$

  • Strains from Airy Stress Function in Plane Stress:

    $$\begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{11} &= \frac{1}{E}\left(\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2} - \nu \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2}\right) \\ \varepsilon_{22} &= \frac{1}{E}\left(\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2} - \nu \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2}\right) \\ \varepsilon_{12} &= -\frac{1+\nu}{E} \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} \\ \varepsilon_{33} &= -\frac{\nu}{E}\left(\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2}\right) = -\frac{\nu}{E} \nabla^2 \Phi \end{aligned}$$

  • Strains from Airy Stress Function in Plane Strain:

    $$\begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{11} &= \frac{1+\nu}{E}\left[(1-\nu)\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2} - \nu \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2}\right] \\ \varepsilon_{22} &= \frac{1+\nu}{E}\left[(1-\nu)\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2} - \nu \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2}\right] \\ \varepsilon_{12} &= -\frac{1+\nu}{E} \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} \\ \varepsilon_{33} &= 0, \quad \varepsilon_{13} = \varepsilon_{23} = 0 \end{aligned}$$

  • Total External Resultant Forces at Edge $x_1 = L$:

    1. Traction on face with normal $\bm{n} = \bm{e}_1$: $\bm{t} = \bm{\sigma}\bm{n} = \sigma_{11} \bm{e}_1 + \sigma_{21} \bm{e}_2$.
    2. Integrated force: $\bm{F} = \int_A \bm{t} \, dA = \int_0^{\text{thickness}} \int_{y_{\min}}^{y_{\max}} \bm{t} \, dx_2 dx_3$.

One-Dimensional Truss Continua

For a slender 1D bar along axis $x_1$ with cross-sectional area $A(x_1)$, under axial stress $\sigma_{11}(x_1)$ and distributed load $\hat{b}_1 = \rho A b_1$:

  • Equilibrium: $\frac{d}{dx_1}(A \sigma_{11}) + \hat{b}_1 = 0$.
  • Constitutive: $\sigma_{11} = E \varepsilon_{11} = E \frac{du_1}{dx_1}$.
  • Governing Displacement ODE: $$\frac{d}{dx_1}\left(E A \frac{du_1}{dx_1}\right) + \hat{b}_1 = 0$$ For constant $E$ and $A$: $E A u_1'' + \hat{b}_1 = 0$.

Problem 1: Tensor Eigenvalues & Kronecker Delta Algebra

Problem Statement:

  1. Given that tensors $\mathbf{R}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ share the same eigenvector $\mathbf{n}$ with corresponding eigenvalues $r$ and $s$ respectively, find an eigenvalue and the corresponding eigenvector for the product tensor $\mathbf{T} = \mathbf{RS}$.
  2. Evaluate the index expression $\delta_{1i}\delta_{ij}\delta_{j2}$.
▸ Show Detailed Solution

Solution:

  1. By definition of eigenvalues and eigenvectors: $$\mathbf{R}\mathbf{n} = r\mathbf{n}, \quad \mathbf{S}\mathbf{n} = s\mathbf{n}$$ Applying tensor $\mathbf{T} = \mathbf{RS}$ to eigenvector $\mathbf{n}$: $$\mathbf{T}\mathbf{n} = (\mathbf{RS})\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{n}) = \mathbf{R}(s\mathbf{n}) = s(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{n}) = s(r\mathbf{n}) = (rs)\mathbf{n}$$ Therefore, an eigenvalue of $\mathbf{T} = \mathbf{RS}$ is $\lambda = rs$, and the corresponding eigenvector is $\mathbf{n}$.

Problem 2: Divergence Theorem Verification on a Finite Cylinder

Problem Statement: Verify Gauss’ Divergence Theorem $\int_{\mathcal{B}} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV = \int_{\partial \mathcal{B}} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dA$ for the cylinder:

$$\mathcal{B}: \quad x_1^2 + x_2^2 \le 1, \quad 0 \le x_3 \le 1$$

under the vector field $F_i = x_3 \delta_{i3}$, which in vector form is $\mathbf{F} = [0, 0, x_3]^T$.

▸ Show Detailed Solution

Solution:

  • Left-Hand Side (Volume Integral): $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = F_{i,i} = \frac{\partial(x_3 \delta_{i3})}{\partial x_i} = \frac{\partial x_3}{\partial x_3} = 1$$ $$\int_{\mathcal{B}} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dV = \int_{\mathcal{B}} 1 \, dV = \text{Volume of Cylinder} = \pi R^2 H = \pi(1)^2(1) = \pi$$
  • Right-Hand Side (Surface Flux Integral): The boundary $\partial\mathcal{B}$ consists of three smooth surfaces:
    1. Lateral surface $S^{(1)}$ ($x_1^2 + x_2^2 = 1, 0 \le x_3 \le 1$): Outward normal $\mathbf{n}^{(1)} = [x_1, x_2, 0]^T$. $$\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n}^{(1)} = 0 \cdot x_1 + 0 \cdot x_2 + x_3 \cdot 0 = 0 \quad \implies \quad \int_{S^{(1)}} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n}^{(1)} \, dA = 0$$
    2. Top surface $S^{(2)}$ ($x_3 = 1, x_1^2 + x_2^2 \le 1$): Outward normal $\mathbf{n}^{(2)} = [0, 0, 1]^T$, where $\mathbf{F} = [0, 0, 1]^T$: $$\int_{S^{(2)}} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n}^{(2)} \, dA = \int_{S^{(2)}} (1) \, dA = \text{Area of Disk} = \pi (1)^2 = \pi$$

  • Total Flux: $$\oint_{\partial \mathcal{B}} \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \, dA = 0 + \pi + 0 = \pi$$ Both sides evaluate identically to $\pi$, confirming the theorem.

Problem 4: Cantilever Beam Airy Stress Function

Problem Statement: A cantilever beam of length $a$ and height $2b$ (occupying $0 \le x_1 \le a$ and $-b \le x_2 \le b$) subjected to end tip force $F$ at $x_1 = 0$ and clamped at $x_1 = a$ is analyzed under plane stress using the Airy stress function:

$$\Phi(x_1, x_2) = \frac{F}{4b^3} \left(x_1 x_2^3 - 3b^2 x_1 x_2\right)$$

Verify biharmonic validity, recover the stress field, and confirm the boundary reaction forces.

▸ Show Detailed Solution

Solution:

  1. Biharmonic Verification: $$\frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_1^4} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_2^4} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2 \partial x_2^2} = 0 \quad \implies \quad \nabla^4 \Phi = 0$$ The function is biharmonically valid throughout the continuum.

Stress Recovery: $$\sigma_{11} = \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2} = \frac{F}{4b^3} (6 x_1 x_2) = \frac{3F}{2b^3} x_1 x_2$$ $$\sigma_{22} = \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2} = 0$$ $$\sigma_{12} = -\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} = -\frac{F}{4b^3} (3x_2^2 - 3b^2) = \frac{3F}{4b^3} (b^2 - x_2^2)$$

  2. Boundary Condition Matching:
    • Vertical Face $AB$ ($x=0$): Normal $\bm{n} = -\bm{e}_1$. Hydrostatic pressure acts inward: $\bm{t} = p(y) \bm{e}_1 = \rho g y \bm{e}_1$. $$\bm{t} = \bm{\sigma}\bm{n} = -\sigma_{11}(0,y)\bm{e}_1 - \sigma_{12}(0,y)\bm{e}_2 = -a_4 y \bm{e}_1 + a_3 y \bm{e}_2$$ Equating components: $$-a_4 y = \rho g y \implies a_4 = -\rho g, \quad a_3 y = 0 \implies a_3 = 0$$
    • Sloped Free Face $AC$ ($x=y$): Unit normal pointing outward is $\bm{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\bm{e}_1 - \bm{e}_2)$. Traction is zero: $$\bm{t} = \bm{\sigma} \bm{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{11} - \sigma_{12} \\ \sigma_{12} - \sigma_{22} \end{bmatrix}_{x=y} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$ Substituting $a_3 = 0$ and $a_4 = -\rho g$: $$\sigma_{11} - \sigma_{12} = (-\rho g y) - (-a_2 x) \xrightarrow{x=y} (a_2 - \rho g)x = 0 \implies a_2 = \rho g$$ $$\sigma_{12} - \sigma_{22} = (-a_2 x) - (a_1 x + a_2 y) \xrightarrow{x=y} -(2a_2 + a_1)x = 0 \implies a_1 = -2a_2 = -2\rho g$$
    • Unique Airy Coefficients: $$\boxed{a_1 = -2\rho g, \quad a_2 = \rho g, \quad a_3 = 0, \quad a_4 = -\rho g}$$
  3. Base Reaction Tractions Along $BC$ ($y=l$): Unit normal pointing into the foundation is $\bm{n} = \bm{e}_2$: $$\bm{t}\big|_{BC} = \bm{\sigma}\bm{e}_2 = \sigma_{12}(x,l)\bm{e}_1 + \sigma_{22}(x,l)\bm{e}_2$$ Substituting the solved coefficients: $$\sigma_{12}(x,l) = -(\rho g x + 0) = -\rho g x$$ $$\sigma_{22}(x,l) = -2\rho g x + \rho g l = \rho g (l - 2x)$$ $$\boxed{\bm{t}\big|_{BC} = -\rho g x \bm{e}_1 + \rho g (l - 2x) \bm{e}_2}$$

7. Beams: Euler-Bernoulli vs. Timoshenko Theory

Theory Overview

Beams are 1D slender structures where length $L$ dominates cross-sectional dimensions $b$ and $h$ ($L \gg b, h$):

  • Timoshenko Beam Theory: Incorporates transverse shear deformation. Valid for thick or moderately thick beams ($L/h \approx 5 - 10$).
  • Euler-Bernoulli Beam Theory: Assumes infinite transverse shear stiffness ($\gamma = 0$). Plane sections remain plane and strictly perpendicular to the deformed neutral axis. Valid for slender beams ($L/h > 10$). Represents the limiting case of Timoshenko theory as shear rigidity $GA_V \to \infty$.

Governing Differential Equations

  • Timoshenko Kinematics: $$u(x,z) = u_0(x) + z \beta(x), \quad w(x,z) = w(x)$$ where $\beta(x)$ is the cross-sectional rotation (independent of deflection slope $w'(x)$).
    • Constitutive Relations: $$N = EA u_0', \quad M = EI \beta', \quad V = GA_V (w' + \beta)$$
    • Static Equilibrium: $$N' + p_x = 0, \quad V' + p_z = 0, \quad M' - V = 0$$
  • Euler-Bernoulli Formulation: Enforces zero transverse shear deformation $\gamma = w' + \beta = 0 \implies \beta(x) = -w'(x)$: $$\boxed{(EI w'')'' = p_z}$$

Systematic Beam Problem-Solving Strategy

Step 1: Identify Theory & Geometry

Determine whether the member is thick ($L/h \le 10$, Timoshenko) or thin ($L/h > 10$, Euler-Bernoulli). Define a right-handed coordinate system with axis $x$ along the beam axis and axis $z$ pointing downwards.

Step 2: Integrate for Internal Forces

Start with the static equilibrium equations:

  • Shear Force $V(x)$: Integrate distributed load $p_z(x)$: $$V(x) = -\int p_z(x) \, dx + C_1$$
  • Bending Moment $M(x)$: Integrate shear force: $$M(x) = \int V(x) \, dx + C_2 = \int \left(-\int p_z \, dx\right) dx + C_1 x + C_2$$
💡
Tip
  • If $p_z = 0$: Shear force $V$ is constant ($C_1$), and bending moment $M$ is linear ($C_1 x + C_2$).
  • If $p_z = \text{const}$: Shear force $V$ is linear, and bending moment $M$ is quadratic.

Step 3: Integrate for Kinematics

  • Case A: Timoshenko Beam (requires 4 integration constants $C_1, C_2, C_3, C_4$):
    1. Rotation $\beta(x)$: Integrate $M = EI \beta'$: $$\beta(x) = \int \frac{M(x)}{EI} \, dx + C_3 = \frac{1}{EI} \int (C_1 x + C_2) \, dx + C_3$$
    2. Deflection $w(x)$: Integrate $V = GA_V(w' + \beta) \implies w' = \frac{V}{GA_V} - \beta$: $$w(x) = \int \left(\frac{V(x)}{GA_V} - \beta(x)\right) dx + C_4$$
  • Case B: Euler-Bernoulli Beam (requires 4 integration constants $C_1, C_2, C_3, C_4$):
    1. Slope $w'(x)$: Integrate $M = -EI w''$: $$w'(x) = -\int \frac{M(x)}{EI} \, dx + C_3$$
    2. Deflection $w(x)$: Integrate slope: $$w(x) = \int w'(x) \, dx + C_4$$

If the distributed load $p_z(x)$ changes abruptly or intermediate supports/point forces exist:

  1. Split the beam into piecewise regions ($0 \le x_1 \le L_1$, $0 \le x_2 \le L_2$).
  2. Formulate integration constants for each region ($C_1^I \dots C_4^I$, $C_1^{II} \dots C_4^{II}$).
  3. Enforce Matching / Interface Conditions at boundary $x_1 = L_1 \leftrightarrow x_2 = 0$:
    • Continuity of Displacements: $w_I(L_1) = w_{II}(0)$, $\beta_I(L_1) = \beta_{II}(0)$.
    • Equilibrium of Internal Forces: $$M_I(L_1) = M_{II}(0) \quad (\text{jump if concentrated external moment is applied})$$ $$V_I(L_1) = V_{II}(0) \quad (\text{jump if concentrated external force is applied})$$
  4. Deformation Decomposition: Total deflection splits cleanly into bending and shear contributions: $$w(x) = w_{\text{bending}}(x) + w_{\text{shear}}(x)$$ where $w_{\text{shear}}$ collects all terms proportional to $\frac{1}{GA_V}$, and $w_{\text{bending}}$ collects all terms proportional to $\frac{1}{EI}$.

8. Plates: Kirchhoff-Love vs. Reissner-Mindlin Theory

Theory Overview

Plates are two-dimensional flat structures ($t \ll L_x, L_y$) carrying out-of-plane transverse loads [mindlin1951] [reissner1945] :

  • Reissner-Mindlin (RM) Plate Theory: Accounts for transverse shear deformation. Normal rotations $\varphi_x, \varphi_y$ are kinematically independent of deflection slopes (the 2D analogue of the Timoshenko beam).
  • Kirchhoff-Love (KL) Plate Theory: Assumes transverse normals remain straight and orthogonal to the deformed midsurface ($\gamma_{xz} = \gamma_{yz} = 0$, so $\varphi_x \approx \frac{\partial w}{\partial x}, \varphi_y \approx \frac{\partial w}{\partial y}$; the 2D analogue of the Euler-Bernoulli beam).

Governing Equations

  • Kirchhoff Plate Bending Rigidity: $$K = \frac{E t^3}{12(1-\nu^2)}$$
  • Classical Biharmonic Plate Equation: $$\boxed{\Delta\Delta w = \frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial x^4} + 2\frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial x^2 \partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial y^4} = \frac{p(x,y)}{K}}$$

Axisymmetric Circular Plates

In polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$, rotationally symmetric loading $p(r)$ causes purely radial deformation $w = w(r)$:

$$\frac{1}{r} \frac{d}{dr} \left\{ r \frac{d}{dr} \left[ \frac{1}{r} \frac{d}{dr} \left( r \frac{dw}{dr} \right) \right] \right\} = \frac{p(r)}{K}$$

Integrating four times successively yields the complete general solution:

$$\boxed{w(r) = C_1 + C_2 r^2 + C_3 \ln r + C_4 r^2 \ln r + w_{\text{particular}}(r)}$$
💡
Tip

Solid Plate Regularity: For solid circular plates without a central hole, deflections and curvatures must remain finite at the center ($r = 0$). Because $\ln r \to -\infty$ as $r \to 0$, boundary regularity requires:

$$C_3 = 0, \quad C_4 = 0$$

Solution Methods for Rectangular Plates

  1. Navier’s Double Fourier Series Method (All Sides Simply Supported): Expand transverse load $p(x,y)$ and deflection $w(x,y)$ in double Fourier sine series: $$w(x,y) = \sum_{m=1}^\infty \sum_{n=1}^\infty w_{mn} \sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{a}\right) \sin\left(\frac{n\pi y}{b}\right)$$ Substituting into $\Delta\Delta w = p/K$ yields algebraic coefficients directly: $$w_{mn} = \frac{p_{mn}}{K \pi^4 \left[\left(\frac{m}{a}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{n}{b}\right)^2\right]^2}$$
  2. Levy’s Single Series Method (Two Opposite Sides Simply Supported): For plates with simple supports at $x = 0$ and $x = a$, assume a single Fourier series: $$w(x,y) = \sum_{m=1}^\infty Y_m(y) \sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{a}\right)$$ This reduces the biharmonic PDE to a set of uncoupled 4th-order ODEs for functions $Y_m(y)$, allowing arbitrary boundary conditions (clamped, free, elastic) along edges $y = 0$ and $y = b$.

9. Saint-Venant Torsion

Theory Overview

Applying twisting moment $M_T$ produces a constant rate of twist $\vartheta' = \frac{d\vartheta}{dx}$:

$$\boxed{M_T = G I_T \vartheta'}$$

where $I_T$ is the torsional constant of the cross-section.

Cross-Sectional Torsion Analysis

1. Solid Circular or Thick Tubular Sections

For circular cross-sections, cross-sections remain planar without out-of-plane warping:

$$I_T = I_p = \frac{\pi r^4}{2}, \quad \tau_{\max} = \frac{M_T r}{I_p}$$

2. Thin-Walled Open Sections (I-beams, Channels, Angles)

Open sections exhibit low torsional stiffness. Composed of $n$ thin rectangular plates of length $b_i$ and thickness $t_i$:

$$I_T \approx \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{3} b_i t_i^3, \quad \tau_{\max} \approx G t_{\max} \vartheta' = \frac{M_T t_{\max}}{I_T}$$

3. Thin-Walled Single-Cell Closed Sections (Bredt’s Theory)

Closed hollow tubes exhibit extremely high torsional stiffness due to circulating shear flow $q = \tau t$:

  • Shear Flow: Constant throughout the closed wall: $$q = \frac{M_T}{2 A_m}$$ where $A_m$ is the cross-sectional area enclosed by the perimeter midline.
  • Shear Stress: $\tau = \frac{q}{t} = \frac{M_T}{2 A_m t}$.
  • Bredt’s Torsional Constant: $$I_T = \frac{4 A_m^2}{\oint \frac{ds}{t}}$$

4. Multi-Cell Thin-Walled Closed Sections

For complex multi-cell cross-sections (e.g., aircraft wings, box girders):

  • Independent circulating shear flows $q_1, q_2, \dots, q_N$ exist in each individual cell.
  • Torque Equilibrium: $$M_T = 2 \sum_{i=1}^N A_i q_i$$
  • Twist Rate Compatibility: The rate of twist $\vartheta'$ must be identical across all cells: $$\vartheta' = \frac{1}{2G A_i} \oint_{\text{cell } i} \frac{q}{t} \, ds \quad (i = 1, \dots, N)$$ Solving this system of linear equations yields the shear flows $q_i$ and overall torsional stiffness.

10. Truss Systems & Matrix Structural Analysis

Theory Overview

Trusses consist of straight slender bars interconnected by frictionless spherical/pin hinges. Bars carry axial forces only ($N_i$).

Static Determinacy Criterion

For a 2D planar truss with $k$ nodes, $s$ member bars, and $r$ support reaction components:

$$f = 2k - s - r$$
  • $f = 0$ (Statically Determinate): Unknown forces can be solved purely from nodal static equilibrium $\sum \bm{F} = \bm{0}$.
  • $f > 0$ (Kinematically Unconstrained / Mechanism): Insufficient members or supports exist; the structure possesses internal kinematic mechanisms.
  • $f < 0$ (Statically Indeterminate): Redundant bars exist ($n = -f$ degrees of indeterminacy); equilibrium alone is insufficient, requiring displacement compatibility.

Problem Solving Strategy for Trusses

Method (A): Determinate Bar Elongation

For any bar $i$ under axial force $N_i$:

$$\Delta l_i = \frac{N_i L_i}{EA}$$

Method (B): Member Unit Vectors

The unit direction vector $\bm{e}_{0,i}$ along bar $i$ directed from node $A$ to node $B$ is:

$$\bm{e}_{0,i} = \frac{1}{L_i} \begin{bmatrix} x_B - x_A \\ y_B - y_A \end{bmatrix}$$

Method (C): Kinematic Displacement-Elongation Relationship

The axial elongation $\Delta l_i$ relates to nodal displacement vectors $\bm{q}_A = [u_A, w_A]^T$ and $\bm{q}_B = [u_B, w_B]^T$ by:

$$\Delta l_i = \bm{e}_{0,i} \cdot (\bm{q}_B - \bm{q}_A) \quad \implies \quad \bm{\Delta l} = \bm{A} \bm{q}$$

where $\bm{A}$ is the kinematic kinematic transformation matrix.

Method (D): Direct Stiffness Method (Indeterminate Systems)

  1. Define Global DOFs: Number unknown displacements at each free node $\bm{u} = [u_1, w_1, u_2, w_2, \dots]^T$.
  2. Element Stiffness Matrix: In global coordinates for a bar inclined at angle $\alpha$ ($c = \cos\alpha, s = \sin\alpha$): $$\mathbf{k}^{(e)} = \frac{EA}{L} \begin{bmatrix} c^2 & cs & -c^2 & -cs \\ cs & s^2 & -cs & -s^2 \\ -c^2 & -cs & c^2 & cs \\ -cs & -s^2 & cs & s^2 \end{bmatrix}$$
  3. Assemble Global Stiffness Matrix: $\mathbf{K} = \sum_{e} \mathbf{T}^{(e)T} \mathbf{k}^{(e)} \mathbf{T}^{(e)}$.
  4. Enforce Boundary Constraints: Eliminate rows and columns corresponding to fixed nodal supports.
  5. Solve Equations: $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{f}$.
  6. Calculate Member Internal Forces: $$S_i = \frac{EA}{L} \left(u_{\text{axial}}^{\text{diff}}\right) = \frac{EA}{L} \left[(u_B - u_A)c + (w_B - w_A)s\right]$$

11. Thin-Walled Shells & the 7-Parameter Model

Theory Overview

Shells are curved thin-walled spatial structures combining:

  • Membrane Action: Load transmission via in-plane normal and shear forces ($N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}$), providing high structural efficiency and stiffness.
  • Bending Action: Secondary resistance via bending moments ($M_{xx}, M_{yy}$) and twisting moments ($M_{xy}$).

Advanced 7-Parameter Shell Kinematics

Standard computational shell elements frequently suffer from numerical shear locking and transverse Poisson thickness locking when 3D constitutive laws are applied. To overcome this, the advanced 7-parameter formulation introduces a quadratic thickness expansion:

$$\boxed{\mathbf{u}(\theta^1, \theta^2, \theta^3) = \mathbf{v}(\theta^1, \theta^2) + \theta^3 \mathbf{w}(\theta^1, \theta^2) + (\theta^3)^2 \widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(\theta^1, \theta^2)}$$
  • $\mathbf{v}$: Midsurface displacement (3 parameters).
  • $\mathbf{w}$: Difference vector / director rotation (3 parameters).
  • $\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}$: Transverse thickness stretch term (1 parameter).

This kinematic expansion allows linear variation of transverse normal strain across the shell thickness, permitting complete, unmodified 3D constitutive equations without artificial numerical locking.

12. Variational Principles & Foundations of FEM

When closed-form analytical solutions of differential equations are intractable, variational methods provide the rigorous foundation for numerical approximations [reddy2006] [hughes1987] .

Principle of Virtual Work (PVW)

A continuous body is in static equilibrium if and only if the internal virtual work equals the external virtual work for every kinematically admissible virtual displacement field $\delta\bm{u}$:

$$\boxed{\delta W_{\text{int}} = \delta W_{\text{ext}}}$$

$$\int_{\mathcal{V}} \bm{\sigma} : \delta\bm{\varepsilon} \, dV = \int_{\mathcal{V}} \rho \bm{b} \cdot \delta\bm{u} \, dV + \int_{\partial\mathcal{V}_t} \bm{t} \cdot \delta\bm{u} \, dA$$

The Rayleigh-Ritz Method

  1. Formulate the Total Potential Energy Functional: $$\Pi(\bm{u}) = U(\bm{u}) - W_{\text{ext}}(\bm{u}) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{V}} \bm{\varepsilon} : \mathbb{C} : \bm{\varepsilon} \, dV - \int_{\mathcal{V}} \rho \bm{b} \cdot \bm{u} \, dV - \int_{\partial\mathcal{V}_t} \bm{t} \cdot \bm{u} \, dA$$
  2. Kinematic Approximation Ansatz: Choose basis functions $\phi_i(x)$ satisfying all essential (kinematic) boundary conditions: $$w(x) \approx \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \phi_i(x)$$
  3. Minimize Potential Energy: $$\frac{\partial \Pi}{\partial c_i} = 0 \quad (i = 1, \dots, n)$$
  4. Solve System: This produces a symmetric linear system of algebraic equations $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{c} = \mathbf{F}$ yielding the energy-optimal approximation coefficients $c_i$.
