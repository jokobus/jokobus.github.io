Structural mechanics provides the rigorous mathematical framework connecting fundamental continuum physics to real-world load-bearing structures. This guide synthesizes two core pillars: Part I: Continuum Mechanics and Three-Dimensional Linear Elasticity, and Part II: Structural Elements (Beams, Plates, Shells, Torsion) and Variational Principles.

Part I: Continuum Mechanics & Linear Elasticity

1. Tensor Calculus Foundations

Scalars, Vectors, and Tensors

In continuum mechanics, physical quantities are invariant under coordinate rotations [timoshenko1970] :

  • Scalars (0th-order): Magnitude only (e.g., mass density $\rho$, temperature $\theta$).
  • Vectors (1st-order): Magnitude and spatial orientation: $\bm{v} = v_i \bm{e}_i$.
  • Second-Order Tensors: Linear operators mapping vectors to vectors: $$\bm{T}(\alpha \bm{u} + \beta \bm{v}) = \alpha \bm{T}\bm{u} + \beta \bm{T}\bm{v}$$
  • Action on Basis Vectors: $\bm{T}\bm{e}_i = T_{ji} \bm{e}_j$, with components: $$[\bm{T}] = \begin{bmatrix} T_{11} & T_{12} & T_{13} \\ T_{21} & T_{22} & T_{23} \\ T_{31} & T_{32} & T_{33} \end{bmatrix} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad T_{ij} = \bm{e}_i \cdot \bm{T} \bm{e}_j$$

Core Tensor Operations

  • Scalar Product (Vectors): $\bm{u} \cdot \bm{v} = u_i v_i$.
  • Vector Product: $\bm{u} \times \bm{v} = \epsilon_{ijk} u_i v_j \bm{e}_k$.
  • Dyadic Product: $(\bm{a} \otimes \bm{b})\bm{v} = (\bm{b} \cdot \bm{v})\bm{a}$, with components $[\bm{a} \otimes \bm{b}]_{ij} = a_i b_j$.
  • Double Contraction (Tensor Inner Product): $$\bm{S} : \bm{D} = \text{tr}(\bm{S}^T \bm{D}) = S_{ij} D_{ij}$$
  • Trace & Determinant: $\text{tr}(\bm{T}) = T_{ii}$ and $\det \bm{T} = \epsilon_{ijk} T_{1i} T_{2j} T_{3k}$ (both frame-invariant).
  • Symmetric & Skew-Symmetric Decomposition: $$\bm{T} = \bm{E} + \bm{W}, \quad \bm{E} = \frac{1}{2}(\bm{T} + \bm{T}^T), \quad \bm{W} = \frac{1}{2}(\bm{T} - \bm{T}^T)$$
  • Orthogonal & Rotation Tensors: $\bm{Q}^T = \bm{Q}^{-1}$ ($\det\bm{Q} = \pm 1$). A proper rotation tensor satisfies $\det\bm{R} = +1$.

Coordinate Transformations

Changing from basis $\bm{e}_i$ to rotated basis $\bm{e}'_i = Q_{mi} \bm{e}_m$:

  • Direction cosines: $Q_{ij} = \bm{e}_i \cdot \bm{e}'_j$.
  • Vector components: $[\bm{v}]_{\bm{e}'} = \bm{Q}^T [\bm{v}]_{\bm{e}}$ ($v'_i = Q_{ki} v_k$).
  • Tensor components: $[\bm{T}]_{\bm{e}'} = \bm{Q}^T [\bm{T}]_{\bm{e}} \bm{Q}$ ($T'_{ij} = Q_{ki} T_{kl} Q_{lj}$).

Principal Values & Invariants

For any symmetric second-order tensor $\bm{S}$, the eigenvalue problem $\bm{S}\bm{n} = \lambda \bm{n}$ yields the characteristic equation:

$$\lambda^3 - I_1 \lambda^2 + I_2 \lambda - I_3 = 0$$

where the principal invariants are:

$$\begin{aligned} I_1(\bm{S}) &= \text{tr}(\bm{S}) = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 \\ I_2(\bm{S}) &= \frac{1}{2}[(\text{tr}\bm{S})^2 - \text{tr}(\bm{S}^2)] = \lambda_1\lambda_2 + \lambda_2\lambda_3 + \lambda_3\lambda_1 \\ I_3(\bm{S}) &= \det \bm{S} = \lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3 \end{aligned}$$

Spectral decomposition expresses the tensor along its principal directions:

$$\bm{S} = \sum_{i=1}^3 \lambda_i (\bm{n}_i \otimes \bm{n}_i) = \bm{Q} \bm{\Lambda} \bm{Q}^T$$

Differential Operators & Integral Theorems

  • Gradient: $\nabla \phi = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x_i} \bm{e}_i$, $\nabla \bm{v} = v_{i,j} \bm{e}_i \otimes \bm{e}_j$.
  • Divergence: $\nabla \cdot \bm{v} = v_{i,i}$, $(\nabla \cdot \bm{S})_i = S_{ij,j}$.
  • Gauss’ Divergence Theorem: $$\int_{\partial \mathcal{B}} \bm{v} \cdot \bm{n} \, dA = \int_{\mathcal{B}} \nabla \cdot \bm{v} \, dV \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \int_{\partial \mathcal{B}} v_i n_i \, dA = \int_{\mathcal{B}} v_{i,i} \, dV$$
  • Stokes’ Theorem: $$\int_{\Gamma} (\nabla \times \bm{v}) \cdot \bm{n} \, dA = \oint_{\partial \Gamma} \bm{v} \cdot \bm{t} \, ds$$

2. Kinematics & Strain Measures

Material vs. Spatial Descriptions

Let $\bm{X}$ denote the reference (material) configuration and $\bm{x} = \bm{\phi}(\bm{X}, t)$ the deformed (spatial) configuration.

  • Displacement: $\bm{u}(\bm{X}, t) = \bm{x} - \bm{X}$.
  • Velocity & Material Derivative: $$\bm{v} = \frac{\partial \bm{\phi}}{\partial t}\Big|_{\bm{X}}, \quad \frac{D(\cdot)}{Dt} = \frac{\partial(\cdot)}{\partial t} + \bm{v} \cdot \nabla(\cdot)$$

Deformation Gradient & Finite Strain

  • Deformation Gradient Tensor: $$\bm{F} = \frac{\partial \bm{x}}{\partial \bm{X}} = \nabla_{\bm{X}} \bm{\phi}, \quad F_{ij} = \frac{\partial x_i}{\partial X_j}$$
  • Volume Ratio (Jacobian): $J = \det\bm{F} > 0$. An isochoric (incompressible) motion satisfies $J=1$.
  • Right Cauchy-Green Tensor: $\bm{C} = \bm{F}^T \bm{F}$ ($C_{IJ} = F_{kI} F_{kJ}$).
  • Green-Lagrange Strain Tensor: $$\bm{E} = \frac{1}{2}(\bm{C} - \bm{I}) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{U} + \nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{U}^T + \nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{U}^T \nabla_{\bm{X}}\bm{U}\right)$$
  • Stretch Ratio $\lambda$ in Direction $\bm{N}$: $\lambda = \frac{\|d\bm{x}\|}{\|d\bm{X}\|} = \sqrt{\bm{N} \cdot \bm{C} \bm{N}}$.

Infinitesimal Strain Tensor

Under the assumption of small displacement gradients ($\|\nabla \bm{u}\| \ll 1$):

$$\bm{\varepsilon} = \frac{1}{2}(\nabla\bm{u} + \nabla\bm{u}^T) \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \varepsilon_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}(u_{i,j} + u_{j,i})$$
  • Normal Strain: $\varepsilon_{nn} = \bm{n} \cdot \bm{\varepsilon} \bm{n} \approx \frac{ds - dS}{dS}$ represents fractional elongation.
  • Engineering Shear Strain: $\gamma_{mn} = 2 \varepsilon_{mn} = 2(\bm{m} \cdot \bm{\varepsilon} \bm{n})$ represents the decrease in the right angle between orthogonal directions $\bm{m}$ and $\bm{n}$.
  • Volumetric Strain: $e = \text{tr}(\bm{\varepsilon}) = \varepsilon_{kk} \approx \frac{\Delta V}{V_0}$.

Saint-Venant Compatibility Conditions

To guarantee that a symmetric strain tensor field $\varepsilon_{ij}(\bm{x})$ can be integrated into a single-valued, continuous displacement field $\bm{u}(\bm{x})$ in a simply-connected domain:

$$\varepsilon_{ij,kl} + \varepsilon_{kl,ij} - \varepsilon_{ik,jl} - \varepsilon_{jl,ik} = 0$$

In 3D Cartesian coordinates, this expands to six independent equations:

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{11}}{\partial x_2^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{22}}{\partial x_1^2} &= 2 \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{12}}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2}, & \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{11}}{\partial x_2 \partial x_3} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x_1} \left( -\frac{\partial \varepsilon_{23}}{\partial x_1} + \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{13}}{\partial x_2} + \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{12}}{\partial x_3} \right) \\ \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{22}}{\partial x_3^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{33}}{\partial x_2^2} &= 2 \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{23}}{\partial x_2 \partial x_3}, & \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{22}}{\partial x_3 \partial x_1} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x_2} \left( \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{23}}{\partial x_1} - \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{13}}{\partial x_2} + \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{12}}{\partial x_3} \right) \\ \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{33}}{\partial x_1^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{11}}{\partial x_3^2} &= 2 \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{13}}{\partial x_3 \partial x_1}, & \frac{\partial^2 \varepsilon_{33}}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial x_3} \left( \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{23}}{\partial x_1} + \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{13}}{\partial x_2} - \frac{\partial \varepsilon_{12}}{\partial x_3} \right) \end{aligned}$$
📝
Note
If all strain components $\varepsilon_{ij}$ are linear functions of coordinates $(x_1, x_2, x_3)$, all second derivatives vanish identically, so compatibility is automatically satisfied.

3. Motion, Equilibrium & Conservation Laws

Cauchy Stress Principle & Traction

Surface force per unit area on a cutting plane with unit normal $\bm{n}$:

$$\bm{t}_{\bm{n}} = \lim_{\Delta A \to 0} \frac{\Delta \bm{F}}{\Delta A} = \bm{\sigma} \bm{n}$$
  • Normal Traction Component: $t_n = \bm{n} \cdot \bm{t}_{\bm{n}} = \bm{n} \cdot \bm{\sigma} \bm{n}$ ($t_n > 0$ tensile, $t_n < 0$ compressive).
  • Shear Traction Component: $t_s = \sqrt{\|\bm{\sigma}\bm{n}\|^2 - (\bm{n} \cdot \bm{\sigma} \bm{n})^2}$.
  • Maximum Shear Stress: $\tau_{max} = \frac{\lambda_{max} - \lambda_{min}}{2}$, acting on planes bisecting the principal stress axes.

Cauchy Stress State on a Differential Element

The conservation of angular momentum requires the Cauchy stress tensor to be symmetric: $\sigma_{ij} = \sigma_{ji}$.

Equations of Linear Momentum (Equilibrium)

Balancing forces on the differential volume yields the Navier-Cauchy equations of motion:

$$\nabla \cdot \bm{\sigma} + \rho \bm{b} = \rho \ddot{\bm{u}} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \sigma_{ij,j} + \rho b_i = \rho \ddot{u}_i$$

For static equilibrium in the absence of body forces ($\bm{b} = \bm{0}$):

$$\sigma_{ij,j} = 0$$

4. Constitutive Laws & Linear Elastic Solids

Generalized Hooke’s Law & Symmetries

For linear elastic materials, the fourth-order elasticity tensor $\mathbb{C}$ relates stress to strain:

$$\sigma_{ij} = C_{ijkl} \varepsilon_{kl}$$
  • Total Components: $3^4 = 81$.
  • Minor Symmetries: Due to $\bm{\sigma} = \bm{\sigma}^T$ and $\bm{\varepsilon} = \bm{\varepsilon}^T$, $C_{ijkl} = C_{jikl} = C_{ijlk} \implies$ 36 components.
  • Major Symmetry (Hyperelasticity): The existence of a strain energy density function $U(\bm{\varepsilon})$ such that $\sigma_{ij} = \frac{\partial U}{\partial \varepsilon_{ij}}$ implies $C_{ijkl} = C_{klij} \implies$ 21 independent components.

Isotropic Linear Elasticity

For materials whose mechanical properties are direction-independent, the 21 constants collapse to just two independent Lamé parameters ($\lambda, \mu$):

$$\sigma_{ij} = \lambda \delta_{ij} \varepsilon_{kk} + 2\mu \varepsilon_{ij} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \bm{\sigma} = \lambda \text{tr}(\bm{\varepsilon})\bm{I} + 2\mu \bm{\varepsilon}$$

Inverting this relationship gives Hooke’s Law in terms of Young’s modulus $E$ and Poisson’s ratio $\nu$:

$$\varepsilon_{ij} = \frac{1+\nu}{E} \sigma_{ij} - \frac{\nu}{E} \sigma_{kk} \delta_{ij} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \bm{\varepsilon} = \frac{1+\nu}{E} \bm{\sigma} - \frac{\nu}{E} \text{tr}(\bm{\sigma})\bm{I}$$
ModulusDefinitionRelationship to Lamé ConstantsPhysical Application
Young’s Modulus ($E$)Tensile/compressive stiffness$E = \frac{\mu(3\lambda + 2\mu)}{\lambda + \mu}$Beams, trusses, columns
Shear Modulus ($G = \mu$)Resistance to shear deformation$G = \mu = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}$Torsion, transverse shear
Bulk Modulus ($K$)Resistance to volume change$K = \lambda + \frac{2}{3}\mu = \frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)}$Hydrostatic pressure, fluids
Poisson’s Ratio ($\nu$)Transverse contraction ratio$\nu = \frac{\lambda}{2(\lambda+\mu)}$Lateral necking, auxetics ($\nu < 0$)

Hydrostatic & Deviatoric Stress Decomposition

Any stress state can be decoupled into volumetric changes and shape distortion:

$$\sigma_{ij} = H_{ij} + S_{ij} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{3}\sigma_{kk} \delta_{ij}}_{\text{Hydrostatic (Volume)}} + \underbrace{\left(\sigma_{ij} - \frac{1}{3}\sigma_{kk}\delta_{ij}\right)}_{\text{Deviatoric (Distortion)}}$$

Navier’s Displacement Equations

Substituting kinematics and constitutive relations into linear momentum equilibrium yields Navier’s equations:

$$\mu \nabla^2 \bm{u} + (\lambda + \mu) \nabla(\nabla \cdot \bm{u}) + \rho \bm{b} = \bm{0} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \mu u_{i,jj} + (\lambda + \mu) u_{j,ij} + \rho b_i = 0$$

5. Two-Dimensional Continua & Airy Stress Functions

Plane Strain vs. Plane Stress

$$\begin{array}{l|c|c} \textbf{Property} & \textbf{Plane Strain (Thick Bodies, Dams, Tunnels)} & \textbf{Plane Stress (Thin Plates, Membranes)} \\ \hline \textbf{Kinematic Constraint} & \varepsilon_{33} = \varepsilon_{13} = \varepsilon_{23} = 0 & \sigma_{33} = \sigma_{13} = \sigma_{23} = 0 \\ \textbf{Out-of-Plane Component} & \sigma_{33} = \nu(\sigma_{11} + \sigma_{22}) \neq 0 & \varepsilon_{33} = -\frac{\nu}{E}(\sigma_{11} + \sigma_{22}) \neq 0 \\ \textbf{Constitutive Matrix} & \bar{E} = \frac{E}{1-\nu^2}, \; \bar{\nu} = \frac{\nu}{1-\nu} & E, \; \nu \text{ (Standard)} \end{array}$$

Airy Stress Function & Biharmonic Equation

For 2D problems with negligible body forces, equilibrium is identically satisfied by defining the stress components via an Airy stress function $\Phi(x_1, x_2)$:

$$\sigma_{11} = \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2}, \quad \sigma_{22} = \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2}, \quad \sigma_{12} = -\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2}$$

Substituting these stresses into the compatibility equation proves that $\Phi$ must satisfy the Biharmonic Equation:

$$\nabla^4 \Phi = \frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_1^4} + 2\frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2 \partial x_2^2} + \frac{\partial^4 \Phi}{\partial x_2^4} = 0$$

6. Worked Benchmark Problems

Benchmark 1: Mars Rover Solar Panel Tractions

▸ Problem Statement & Derivation

Problem: The stress field in the solar panel of a planetary rover is modeled by the quadratic Airy stress function:

$$\Phi(x_1, x_2) = a_1 x_1^2 + a_2 x_1 x_2 + a_3 x_2^2$$

1. Biharmonic Check: Fourth derivatives vanish identically, so $\nabla^4 \Phi = 0$ is satisfied.
2. Stress Recovery:

$$\sigma_{11} = \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_2^2} = 2a_3, \quad \sigma_{22} = \frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1^2} = 2a_1, \quad \sigma_{12} = -\frac{\partial^2 \Phi}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} = -a_2$$

3. Boundary Tractions: On boundaries with normal $\bm{n}$, $\bm{t} = \bm{\sigma}\bm{n}$.

Benchmark 2: Hydrostatic Gravity Dam

▸ Problem Statement & Derivation

Problem: A triangular dam under plane strain is subjected to hydrostatic water pressure $p(y) = \rho g y$ on its vertical face $AB$ ($x=0$), with free surface $AC$ ($x=y$):

$$\Phi(x, y) = \frac{1}{6}a_1 x^3 + \frac{1}{2}a_2 x^2 y + \frac{1}{2}a_3 x y^2 + \frac{1}{6}a_4 y^3$$

Matching boundary conditions reveals the unique coefficients:

$$a_1 = -2\rho g, \quad a_2 = \rho g, \quad a_3 = 0, \quad a_4 = -\rho g$$

The resulting base reaction traction on $BC$ ($y=l$) is:

$$\bm{t}\big|_{BC} = -\rho g x \bm{e}_1 + \rho g (l - 2x) \bm{e}_2$$

Part II: Structural Elements & Variational Principles

7. Beam Formulations: Euler-Bernoulli vs. Timoshenko

1D structural models approximate slender structural members ($L \gg h, b$).

Kinematic Hypotheses

  • Timoshenko Beam Theory (Thick/Shear-Deformable):
    Plane sections remain plane but not necessarily perpendicular to the deformed neutral axis. Cross-sectional rotation $\beta(x)$ is independent of deflection slope $w'(x)$:

    $$u(x,z) = u_0(x) + z\beta(x), \quad w(x,z) = w(x)$$

    $$\text{Shear Strain: } \gamma = w'(x) + \beta(x)$$

    $$\text{Constitutive: } N = EA u_0', \quad M = EI \beta', \quad V = GA_V (w' + \beta)$$

    $$\text{Equilibrium: } N' + p_x = 0, \quad V' + p_z = 0, \quad M' - V = 0$$

  • Euler-Bernoulli Beam Theory (Thin/Shear-Rigid):
    Plane sections remain plane and strictly perpendicular to the neutral axis ($\gamma = 0 \implies \beta(x) = -w'(x)$):

    $$(EI w'')'' = p_z$$

Systematic Integration Procedure

  1. Equilibrium Integration: $$V(x) = -\int p_z(x) dx + C_1$$ $$M(x) = \int V(x) dx + C_2$$
  2. Kinematic Integration:
    • Timoshenko: $\beta(x) = \int \frac{M(x)}{EI} dx + C_3$, $\quad w(x) = \int \left(\frac{V(x)}{GA_V} - \beta(x)\right) dx + C_4$.
    • Euler-Bernoulli: $w'(x) = -\int \frac{M(x)}{EI} dx + C_3$, $\quad w(x) = \int w'(x) dx + C_4$.
  3. Boundary Condition Enforcement:

8. Plate Theory: Kirchhoff-Love vs. Reissner-Mindlin

Plates are 2D flat structures ($t \ll L_x, L_y$) resisting transverse loads primarily through internal bending moments ($M_{xx}, M_{yy}$), twisting moments ($M_{xy}$), and transverse shears ($Q_x, Q_y$) [mindlin1951] [reissner1945] .

  • Kirchhoff Bending Stiffness: $K = \frac{Et^3}{12(1-\nu^2)}$.
  • Biharmonic Plate Equation: $$\Delta\Delta w = \frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial x^4} + 2\frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial x^2 \partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^4 w}{\partial y^4} = \frac{p(x,y)}{K}$$

Axisymmetric Circular Plates

Under rotationally symmetric loads $p(r)$, the plate equation simplifies in polar coordinates:

$$\frac{1}{r} \frac{d}{dr} \left\{ r \frac{d}{dr} \left[ \frac{1}{r} \frac{d}{dr} \left( r \frac{dw}{dr} \right) \right] \right\} = \frac{p(r)}{K}$$

Integrating four times yields the general solution:

$$w(r) = C_1 + C_2 r^2 + C_3 \ln r + C_4 r^2 \ln r + w_{\text{particular}}(r)$$

For solid circular plates without a central hole, $C_3 = C_4 = 0$ to avoid logarithmic singularity at $r = 0$.

Classical Plate Boundary Conditions

$$\begin{array}{l|l|l} \textbf{Edge Type} & \textbf{Kirchhoff-Love (KL)} & \textbf{Reissner-Mindlin (RM)} \\ \hline \textbf{Clamped} & w = 0, \quad \frac{\partial w}{\partial n} = 0 & w = 0, \quad \varphi_n = 0, \quad \varphi_s = 0 \\ \textbf{Simply Supported} & w = 0, \quad M_n = 0 & w = 0, \quad M_n = 0, \quad \varphi_s = 0 \text{ (Hard)} \\ \textbf{Free Edge} & M_n = 0, \quad V_{\text{eff}} = Q_n + \frac{\partial M_{ns}}{\partial s} = 0 & M_n = 0, \quad M_{ns} = 0, \quad Q_n = 0 \end{array}$$

9. Saint-Venant Torsion

Applying a twisting moment $M_T$ generates a constant rate of twist $\vartheta' = \frac{d\vartheta}{dx}$:

$$M_T = G I_T \vartheta'$$

Cross-Sectional Torsion Constants

$$\begin{array}{l|l|l} \textbf{Cross-Section Type} & \textbf{Torsional Constant } I_T & \textbf{Peak Shear Stress } \tau_{max} \\ \hline \textbf{Solid Circular / Tube} & I_T = I_p = \frac{\pi r^4}{2} & \tau_{max} = \frac{M_T r}{I_p} \\ \textbf{Thin-Walled Open (I-beam, C-channel)} & I_T \approx \sum \frac{1}{3} b_i t_i^3 & \tau_{max} \approx G t_{max} \vartheta' = \frac{M_T t_{max}}{I_T} \\ \textbf{Thin-Walled Closed (Bredt Box)} & I_T = \frac{4 A_m^2}{\oint \frac{ds}{t}} & \tau = \frac{q}{t} = \frac{M_T}{2 A_m t} \end{array}$$

where $A_m$ is the area enclosed by the mean wall perimeter line.

10. Truss Systems & Matrix Structural Analysis

Trusses are networks of pin-jointed bars carrying axial forces only.

Static Determinacy Criterion

For a 2D planar truss with $k$ nodes, $s$ members (bars), and $r$ support reactions:

$$f = 2k - s - r$$
  • $f = 0$: Statically determinate. Solved via nodal equilibrium.
  • $f < 0$: Statically indeterminate. Requires the direct stiffness method.

Element Stiffness Matrix in 2D Global Coordinates

For a bar inclined at angle $\alpha$ with $c = \cos\alpha$ and $s = \sin\alpha$:

$$\mathbf{k}^{(e)} = \frac{EA}{L} \begin{bmatrix} c^2 & cs & -c^2 & -cs \\ cs & s^2 & -cs & -s^2 \\ -c^2 & -cs & c^2 & cs \\ -cs & -s^2 & cs & s^2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Assembling into the global system $\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{f}$ and enforcing boundary constraints yields the nodal displacement vector $\mathbf{u}$.

11. Thin-Walled Shells & the 7-Parameter Model

Shells combine membrane action (in-plane forces $N_{\alpha\beta}$, highly efficient) with bending action (moments $M_{\alpha\beta}$, flexible). In modern computational finite element formulations, standard shell elements suffer from shear locking and Poisson thickness locking.

The advanced 7-parameter shell model expands displacement kinematics:

$$\bm{u}(\theta^1, \theta^2, \theta^3) = \bm{v}(\theta^1, \theta^2) + \theta^3 \bm{w}(\theta^1, \theta^2) + (\theta^3)^2 \widetilde{\bm{w}}(\theta^1, \theta^2)$$

This formulation accounts for continuous transverse thickness stretching, enabling complete 3D constitutive laws without artificial artificial numerical locking.

12. Energy Principles & Foundations of the Finite Element Method

When exact closed-form solutions of differential equations are intractable, variational methods provide rigorous approximations [reddy2006] [hughes1987] .

Principle of Virtual Work

For any kinematically admissible virtual displacement $\delta\bm{u}$:

$$\delta W_{\text{int}} = \delta W_{\text{ext}} \quad \leftrightarrow \quad \int_{\mathcal{V}} \bm{\sigma} : \delta\bm{\varepsilon} \, dV = \int_{\mathcal{V}} \bm{b} \cdot \delta\bm{u} \, dV + \int_{\partial \mathcal{V}_t} \bm{t} \cdot \delta\bm{u} \, dA$$

The Rayleigh-Ritz Method

  1. Formulate the total potential energy functional: $\Pi(\bm{u}) = U(\bm{u}) - W_{\text{ext}}(\bm{u})$.
  2. Assume an ansatz fulfilling all essential (kinematic) boundary conditions: $$w(x) \approx \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \phi_i(x)$$
  3. Minimize potential energy with respect to generalized coordinates $c_i$: $$\frac{\partial \Pi}{\partial c_i} = 0 \quad (i = 1, \dots, n)$$
  4. Solving the resulting linear algebraic system yields the best energy-optimal polynomial approximation to the true continuum deformation.
